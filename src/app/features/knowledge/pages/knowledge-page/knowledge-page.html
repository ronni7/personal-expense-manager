Reference

Financial glossary

Fixed expense
A recurring cost that is usually stable, such as rent, internet, or insurance.
Variable expense
A cost that changes month to month, such as groceries, fuel, or entertainment.
Emergency fund
Cash set aside to cover sudden costs without needing to take on debt.
Savings rate
The portion of income you save or invest after taxes and essential spending.
Discretionary spending
Flexible spending on wants rather than needs, which is often the easiest category to review.

Quick answers

Why this matters

Why track irregular expenses?

Small, uneven costs can accumulate quickly. Tracking them helps you plan for the months when they are more common.

Why separate fixed and variable costs?

Fixed costs are easier to predict, while variable costs can reveal hidden leakage in the budget. Separating them makes review more actionable.

Why review a budget monthly?

Life changes, income shifts, and seasonal spending rise and fall. A monthly check-in helps you course-correct before small issues become bigger ones.

Quick habits

Useful note

Tip: If a category is hard to forecast, reduce the number of decisions you make each month by setting a cap and reviewing it weekly.
